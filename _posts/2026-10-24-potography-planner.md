As someone very smart once said 'everything should be made as simple as possible, but no simpler.' Needless to say, then, this handy little tool by a fellow enthusiast imediatly caught my attention.

### Bringing Order to Creative Chaos

If there's is one thing us snappers have in common, it's this: we love being out taking photos far more than we love planning for it. The reality, however, is that successful photography often depends on preparation; weather, light, tides, sunrise times, travel distances, shooting priorities... there's a lot to consider.

In this [video by Darren Arnold](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=3Cq6ri2weSo&pp=0gcJCbgCo7VqN5tD), we're introduced to a free Photography Day Planner built in Google Sheets to help tackle this very problem. The planner brings together key information such as weather forecasts, sunrise and sunset times, and other planning data into a single workspace designed specifically for photographers. 


### The Planning Problem

Photography planning can quickly become fragmented&nbsp;— one browser tab contains weather forecasts, another shows sunrise and sunset times, and yet another displays location information or route planning. The result is information overload, and photographers can find themselves juggling multiple sources of data and trying to make sense of it all instead of concentrating on composition, storytelling, and creativity.

### A Photographer's Dashboard

What immediately appeals about this planner is the concept of centralisation; rather than constantly switching between websites and apps, the planner aims to place the most important decision-making information into a single view&nbsp;— weather forecasts, sunrise and sunset data, and daily photography considerations can all be reviewed together  (and for landscape photographers in particular, this can be very useful indeed).

### Improving Creative Efficiency

One of the hidden benefits of planning is that it frees mental bandwidth. Many photographers worry that planning might somehow reduce spontaneity; in practice, the opposite is often true&nbsp;— the less time spent wondering where to go or whether conditions will be suitable, the more attention can be devoted to observation, experimentation, and creativity.

### Balancing Structure and Flexibility

Of course, no planner can predict everything; weather changes, light behaves unexpectedly, and new opportunities appear without warning. The true value of a planning system, however, is not that it creates a rigid itinerary; its true value lies in providing a structured starting point.


### Why Simple Tools Often Win

There's often a temptation to assume that the best photographic workflow requires specialist software, subscriptions, and increasingly complex systems.
Sometimes, though, a much simpler spreadsheet is absolutely enough&nbsp;— and the planner showcased in the video embraces this philosophy by using a straightforward format while bringing together information that photographers commonly need.

### Final Thoughts

The Photography Day Planner demonstrates an important truth: successful photography is often a combination of creativity and preparation.

Great images still require vision, patience, and technical skill&nbsp;— but planning can dramatically increase the chances of being in the right place at the right time.

Whether you use a dedicated planner, a notebook, or your own customised spreadsheet, the principle remains the same: eliminating uncertainty isn't the goal; the goal is to spend less time managing logistics and more time making photographs.
