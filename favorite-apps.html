My comments, thoughts, and opinions are based on personal experience only; they should not be considered definitive — so don’t go blaming me if your drive explodes, Bob!

Git

Git is a free and open-source version-control system that can be used for so much more than just software development — for example, I often use Git to manage such things as my RawTherapee PP3s and processing work (it's a very diverse and powerful tool).

Jekyll

Jekyll is a free and open-source tool that's absolutely indispensable for creating static websites (such as this one, for example). GitHub Pages is actually powered by Jekyll (with loads of integrated plugins and fully supported documentation).

DNGLab

Adobe DNG Converter doesn’t work on Linux (you can try to run it in Wine, of course, but it’s far from an ideal solution). Enter DNGLab: a camera-raw to DNG file converter that runs in the Linux command line; it’s easy to install, simple to use, and it’s free and open-source.

Lensfun

Lenses aren’t perfect; chromatic aberations, geometric distortion, vignetting... all of them need correcting for in post. Lensfun is a free and open-source library of preset optical corrections (and with a little patience and practice, you can even create your own).

RawTherapee

If your looking for a competent raw processor, there’s no need to look any further. I’ve been using RawTherapee since I first held a digital camera; it’s multi-platform, it’s packed with useful features, and it’s completely free and open-source (it also supports some terminal commands).

FEH

When you need to check photos on the run, a bloat-free, resource-light, and lighting-quick viewer is king. This is where FEH comes in; just set up a shortcut in your toolbar (configuration is as easy as it is extensive), and you’re good to go. FEH is both free and open-source.

ExifTool

I sometimes like a little privacy. 😉 ExifTool is both a Perl library and a command-line utility that, amongst other things, reads and writes to the metadata of media files (third-party GUIs are also available); it’s completely platform-independent, and it’s free and open-source.

FFmpeg

When it comes to multimedia files, nothing beats FFmpeg; from encoding and decoding to streaming and filtering, I’ve yet to find a format that isn’t fully supported. FFmpeg is a cross-platform command-line project that’s free and open-source (though a few GUIs are also available).

PeaZip

When it comes to archive management, nothing quite matches PeaZip; it’s cross-platform, free and open-source, supports almost every format available, and has a host of jucy features that are simply unmatched in it’s field (including, not least of all, several advanced security options).

rsync

Backups are really important, but they’re often a bit of a faff; rsync, a free and open-source command-line tool for syncing files on the fly, takes away all of the pain (there’s also a GUI you can use should you need to). From PP3s to final edits, Rclone can sync both locally and remotely.

Rclone

Backups are really important, but they’re often a bit of a faff (so much so, that I had to say it twice 😉). Rclone is much like rsync, but it backs up straight to the cloud (think Dropbox, for example); it’s a comand-line tool that’s free and open source (but, once again, GUIs are also available).
